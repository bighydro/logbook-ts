# Changelog

All notable changes to logbook-ts. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/);
the versions are this implementation's, and the spec it follows is named in each entry.

## Unreleased

### Added
- `rollup nights <root> [--year YYYY | --since YYYY-MM-DD --until YYYY-MM-DD] [--json]`: per year of the window, the nights at home — a place of kind `home` holding the night's stay, or within 400 m of its centre (docs/day.md's home-region rule) —, away and in transit, the nights aboard each asset by id, and the longest trip — the longest run of consecutive nights not at home, nights in transit counted in, clipped to the year, the earlier of two as long, none when every night was at home — as the reference's `logbook rollup nights` prints it (docs/rollups.md, *Countries, flights, nights*; ADR 0019). Without a place of kind `home` every night with a stay is away and the rollup says so. Under `--json` every number carries the first and last point of each night's stay. `rollupNights` and `renderNights` in the library.
- `day` carries the spend line the reference's Day has since its ledger (03a8c98, 2026-10-03; docs/ledger.md, *The Day*): `spend` under `--json` — `null` with no `transaction/v1` line on the day, else `count`, `deleted`, `totals` per currency (`spent`, `received`, `net`), `merchants` and `lines` — and, in the text, `spend  CHF -94.50 · NOK 44,727.00 · 6 transactions · 1 deleted · Kafé Måken, Gasthaus zur Brücke +1` after the health line. A correction that `supersedes` a line stands in its place, a retracted line is out, a line the source marks deleted is listed and counted as deleted but never summed, and the day is the local day of `at` (SPEC-QUESTIONS 69). The expected-day files are re-captured.
- `tests/fixtures/nights-sample`: eleven synthetic days over the turn of 2025–26 with the cases the nights rollup adds (`make.mjs`: a year with every night at home, nights aboard two yachts with the one boarded later having more, two runs away of three nights each so the longest trip is a tie), with the reference's `trips`, `rollup countries` and `rollup nights` output beside it.
- `tests/fixtures/demo-seed1`: the reference's own `logbook demo --seed 1 --out`, a month of a person in Oslo who does not exist (12,772 lines, `places.json`, `assets.json`, `policy/`, two notes, two attachments; `index.sqlite` left out), committed whole so that the cross-implementation diff covers a record of the reference's making, with its `trips`, `rollup countries` and `rollup nights` for the whole month, the year and a fortnight. The conformance sample (`tests/fixtures/sample-logbook`) has the same three beside its `expected-show/`: no home place, eight nights in transit.
- `tests/cross-impl.test.ts` diffs `rollup nights` beside `trips` and `rollup countries`, text and JSON, on every fixture with expectations — the two probe records, the two day fixtures, the conformance sample and the seed-1 demo — and on the seed-7 demo record, so a divergence from the reference fails CI; `tests/fixtures/capture-expected-trips.mjs` captures `expected-nights/` too.
- SPEC-QUESTIONS 67–69: what the longest trip is and how it differs from `trips`, the counts, text and lines of the nights rollup, and three things found beside it (the order of the nights aboard, the trip id in the text, the Day's spend line).
- `days <root> [--from YYYY-MM-DD] [--to YYYY-MM-DD] [--json]`: a window of days read back one line each, as the reference's `logbook days` prints it (docs/day.md, *A window of days*; SPEC §3.2.7): the date and weekday; the night after with the country when it is away, `in transit` when no stay reaches it, `no location` when the day has no location line at all; the kilometres of every move that started on the day, the passages aboard an asset included; the flights; the stays — a stay or a run aboard, never a stop — with how many lines attached across the day's rows, or `nothing logged`; the people confirmed present; the health line; and a gap marker naming every usual source silent that day, a source being usual when it has a line on at least four in five of the window's days that have any line. `--json` prints JSON Lines, one object per day. Both bounds default to the days the track covers, else the days with any line; a bound given beyond them is taken as it is. The window is read a calendar month at a time, each with the day before it, and the stays are derived over that reading, as the reference does, so a run aboard that spans days is one row on each and keeps its start. The Day reader is split for it into `openDayReader`, `deriveRows` and `dayOf`, `readDay` unchanged. `readDayRows`, `renderDayRow`, `renderDayRows` and `kilometresText` in the library.
- `tests/fixtures/demo-seed1/make.mjs` rebuilds the vendored seed-1 demo record from the reference's `logbook demo --seed 1` — the record SPEC §6.1 compares two implementations on, thirty days from 2026-06-01, 12,772 lines, nothing in it real — and `tests/cross-impl.test.ts` checks the vendored record against a fresh run of the reference. `expected-days/` beside it, beside the conformance sample and beside every other fixture is the reference's `days` output for the whole record and for ranges beyond and inside the track, captured by `tests/fixtures/capture-expected-readers.mjs` and never edited by hand; the unit tests and the cross-implementation test diff ours against it.
- `people <root> [--year YYYY] [--json]`: everyone the record's resolution lines name as a person, never the owner, and what the record knows of each in the window, as the reference's `logbook people` prints it (docs/people.md): the channels they are heard on with how many lines, first and last day, highest tier and last line — messages they sent and the owner's in a direct chat with them, calls, mail from or to them, calendar entries they attend and did not decline, transcripts they took part in (by ref, else by a name the record labels exactly one person by), tagged faces — the days together of the with module's confirmed set only, the nights whose overnight stay they were confirmed at on that day, the places shared by days there (a named place, `aboard <asset>` by id, else the coordinates with what is near), the first and last contact and the last real one (a meeting first on its day, else a message or an answered call; a mail never), their refs, tier and birthday; one row per person, most days together first, under a head with the window and the report's tier. The window is the record's days with a line of any kind, or one year clipped to it (`openWindow` takes `coverage: "lines"`). The window reader keeps, per stay and per run aboard, the evidence it read the company from (`WindowRow.units`, `evidenceOf` and `mergeCompany` split out of `company`), and the resolver lists every ref that reaches an entity (`refsOf`). `expected-people/` beside every fixture is the reference's output, diffed by the unit tests, the CLI tests and `tests/cross-impl.test.ts`. `readPeople`, `renderPeople` and `CHANNELS` in the library.
- SPEC-QUESTIONS 70–73: the `days` row and JSON, the month-wide reading behind it, the gap rule and the window bound, as the reference's output shows them; 74–76: the people report's text and JSON, the channel rules the prose leaves open, and what a day together is read from.
- `trips <root> [--year YYYY | --since YYYY-MM-DD --until YYYY-MM-DD] [--json]`: the trips of a window, as the reference's `logbook trips` prints them (README "Trips are derived, never written", ADR 0019, docs/rollups.md): a trip is a run of consecutive days whose night is away from home — the night is the stay with the longest part in the night window, home when it lies in a place of kind `home` or within 400 m of one whatever the radius — or in transit, with at least one night at a stay; a night the tracker slept through joins the trip on either side, and a run of such nights alone is no trip. Each trip has its derived id `trip:<first day>:<last day>`, its nights (aboard an asset when every night with a stay was aboard it, else counted per asset and in transit), its route (the night places in order, consecutive nights at one place as one: the named place, `aboard <asset>`, the airport's `CPH, Copenhagen`, the coordinates `near <place>, x km`, the coordinates with the nearest large airport's city, or the coordinates alone), the named places stayed at from the first day to the day after, the people confirmed present there (at most twelve, most evidence first and then by name, each with the lines that put them there), the flights of its first day (`in`), of the day after (`out`) and in between, and under `--json` the lines behind every number. The window is the whole record, one year or a range, clipped to the days the owner's track covers; without a place of kind `home` the command says so. `readTrips`, `renderTrips` and `labelOf` in the library.
- `rollup countries <root> [--year YYYY | --since YYYY-MM-DD --until YYYY-MM-DD] [--json]`: days per country per year from the overnight stay — a place's own `country` when the stay lies in one, else the zone of the nearest large airport within 300 km — most days first; nights in transit and nights no airport is near apart; the method printed with the numbers; the dates, the lines and the count by method under `--json`. `rollupCountries` and `renderCountries` in the library.
- Both read the window in one pass over the month files it can touch, with the memory of one month's lines and what is open (`openWindow`, `readDays`): the stays engine now derives segment by segment as the points arrive (`SegmentStream`, which `deriveSegments` is built on), a segment is marked aboard an asset once the asset's fixes around it are in, consecutive segments aboard one asset fold into one row, and a day is produced as soon as every row that can touch its night has settled. `tests/reading.test.ts` holds the high-water mark of an eight-month record to that of a two-month one.
- An asset's position at an owner's instant is read between the asset's two fixes around it when they are at most twice `aboard_window_s` apart, else from the nearest fix within the window (docs/day.md, *Aboard an asset*), so an AIS fix every ten minutes still places a passage; before, only the nearest fix was compared.
- `tests/fixtures/trips-sample`: seventeen synthetic days over the turn of 2025–26 with a case of every rule the two readers follow (`make.mjs`: a trip across the year boundary with a flight in, in the middle and out, a night at an airport hotel, a stop and a visit at named places, a confirmed person's photos and a face tagged the day after, thirteen guests, a run aboard a yacht, a night 600 m from home and one 300 m from it, nights the tracker slept through, a camp no airport is near); `expected-trips/` and `expected-countries/` beside it, beside `day-sample` and beside `demo-sample` are the reference's output for the whole record, a year and a range, captured by `tests/fixtures/capture-expected-trips.mjs` and never edited by hand; `tests/cross-impl.test.ts` diffs both readers against the reference on every one of them and on the whole demo record (`logbook demo --days 30 --seed 7`: four trips, three countries).
- SPEC-QUESTIONS 58–66: the rules of a trip and of the countries rollup in the detail the prose leaves out, and where the reference's `day` has moved on since the day fixtures were captured.
- `day <root> [YYYY-MM-DD] [--json]`: the day read back whole, as the reference's `logbook day` prints it (docs/day.md): the nights either side, the country, the timeline of stays, stops, moves, gaps, runs aboard an asset and flights with what attached to each (events, transcripts, notes, mail threads, calls, keepers; messages and photos counted) and who was there (confirmed by a calendar entry held at the stay, a transcript, a note's `with`; proposed by a tagged face; never the owner), what was placed nowhere, the health line and the sources. Stays are derived from the `location/v1` points by the documented rules (20 min or evidence, a place's radius, excursions and silences, the 400 m home rule, the gap rule) with `policy/stays.json`'s thresholds; `aboard` from the asset tracks of `assets.json`; airports and countries from the reference's own tables (`src/tables.ts`, generated by `scripts/make-tables.mjs`). `--json` prints the same Day as the reference. Matched by running the reference on `tests/fixtures/day-sample` (six synthetic days with a case of every rule) and on its demo record (`logbook demo --days 30 --seed 7`; three days vendored as `tests/fixtures/demo-sample`); `expected-day/` beside each is the reference's output, checked by the unit tests and by `tests/cross-impl.test.ts`. `readDay` and `renderDay` in the library, with `deriveSegments`, `markAboard` and the settings readers.
- SPEC-QUESTIONS 42–54: the Day's text and JSON, the stay rules in the detail the prose leaves out, aboard, labels, the country, people, attachments, health, sources, the window read, and the two places the reference departs from docs/day.md.
- `stats [--json]`: one screen of what the record holds, as the reference prints it — format and head; lines, first and last `at`; lines per kind with first and last local day and distinct sources; per source; per local year as a bar of one character per percent of the busiest year; retractions and the distinct lines they name; resolution lines and the distinct entities they mint; attachments referenced under `payload.media`, `payload.extra.media` or `payload.content` and how many are present under `attachments/`; what the run took — plus two tables of this implementation's own, lines per tier and per local month. Matched to the reference by running it on the fixtures and on its demo record, never by reading it (SPEC-QUESTIONS 55). One pass over every month file; what is held is one counter per distinct kind, source, month, entity and digest, never the lines. `collectStats` and `statsText` in the library.
- `sources --gaps [--since YYYY-MM-DD] [--expect <source>…] [--json]`: where each source went quiet, as the reference prints it — per source with lines in the range, the lines, its last line's local time, the longest silence (between two lines, from the range's start to the first line under `--since`, or since the last line and still running) and the local days with no line, counted and folded into runs; the range from each source's first line, or `--since`, to today in the record's zone, a line dated after today outside it, today missing only once the source has been silent a full day; `--expect` lists the sources named in that order, marks one silent a day or more, or with no line, with `!`, and exits 1 when any is; `--since` after today, a malformed day, or `--since`, `--expect` or `--json` without `--gaps` exit 2 with the reference's messages (SPEC-QUESTIONS 56). Streamed: one file's timestamps per source at a time, sorted before the gaps are measured. `sources` alone lists the record's sources with their lines and first and last line, since this implementation has no adapters to list (SPEC-QUESTIONS 57). `sourceGaps`, `gapsText`, `listSources` and `sourcesText` in the library; `main` takes `{ now }` so a test can fix the clock.
- The cross-implementation test diffs `stats`, `stats --json`, `sources --gaps` (with and without `--since`, as JSON, and with `--expect`) against the reference on the three fixtures and on the reference's own demo record (`logbook demo`, 12,772 lines), which it writes into a temp folder when `LOGBOOK_REF` is set.
- `show --since YYYY-MM-DD --until YYYY-MM-DD`: a range of local days, inclusive, oldest first, each day as `--day` prints it and a blank line between; a day without a line is left out, an empty range prints `<since>–<until>: nothing logged`, and a missing bound is the record's first or last listed day. The reader streams: one pass over every file for the judgements (resolutions, retractions, superseded flights) and the record's first and last day, then only the month files the range can touch, in order, a day printed as soon as the last file that can hold one of its lines has been read; what is held is the days still open, at most a month's worth, so memory does not grow with the record. `showRange` and `showDays` in the library.
- `show --profile <schema>` (repeatable, or comma-separated): only the lines whose `payload.schema` is one of the profiles given; `note` means every version of `note/vN`. Applied before runs of points are collapsed and calendar entries folded; resolutions and retractions still come from the whole record. `profiles` on `showDay`, `showDays` and `showRange`.
- `show --json`: each day as one JSON object on one line — `day`, `timezone`, `hero`, `rows`, `note` — every row with the text column as `summary` (names resolved, or raw with `--raw`), its local `time`, `until` for a run, `kind`, `source` (every source of a folded entry), `retraction` for a hidden line, and `lines`, the full lines behind the row. `detail` on every `showDay` and `showDays` result.
- `show` prints a day's hero photos — `  hero  IMG_0001.jpg` under the heading, the `memory` lane first and every other lane as `(art)` — and a `keeper/v1` line (RFC 0024) as `hero photo (<lane>): <photo>`, as the reference does (SPEC-QUESTIONS 38).
- The conformance sample is part of the cross-implementation diff: `tests/fixtures/sample-logbook/expected-show/` holds the reference's output for every one of its eight days, checked by the unit tests and by `tests/cross-impl.test.ts`. 2026-03-01 and 2026-03-06 came in once the reference (b3cd8c5, PR #138, 2026-10-02) read them as SPEC §3.2 says: the run of points ends at the last point's `end`, a string `chat` is a direct chat's name, and the generic row spells numbers by value (SPEC-QUESTIONS 24, 25, 27 and 40, now answered).

### Fixed
- A `logbook/0.3` record is read, verified and summed up as it is: it hashes by the same rule as 0.2 (SPEC §3.1, ADR 0020), so `verify` checks its whole chain without a key, a sealed line's `payload_enc` preserved as an unknown field and never opened, and every reader reads it; before, every command refused any format but 0.2, and the reference's `logbook demo` writes 0.3 since its #143 (cd35015, 2026-10-04), so the cross-implementation job failed on every demo-record case. `add` still writes 0.2 lines: it appends to a 0.3 record that names no `recipients` (such a record seals nothing) and refuses one that names them, since nothing here can seal.
- The window of `trips`, `rollup countries` and `rollup nights` is clipped to the first and last local day with a location line of any subject, an asset's AIS fix included, as the reference's is: the conformance sample's last day holds only the yacht's fix, and the window runs to it. Before, only the owner's own lines bounded it (SPEC-QUESTIONS 64, corrected).
- `trips` orders the nights aboard several assets by asset id in the text and the JSON (`(1 aboard Alpha, 2 aboard Zeta)`), as the reference does, not by the order boarded.
- The JCS property test's own reference (`tests/jcs.test.ts`, a `JSON.stringify` with a property list) leaked a top-level `__proto__` key whenever a nested object owned a `__proto__` property, failing the suite on one random seed in about a thousand (#5). It now copies the tree into prototype-less objects first; the failing draw is replayed by seed and path on every run, beside the same shape built with `JSON.parse`.

### Changed
- The Day and every trip carry `weather: null` under `--json`, the field the reference added with `sync weather` (a4a9b0c, 2026-10-03; RFC 0026). Only null is read: no fixture has a `weather/v1` line, and a record with them is a reader of its own (SPEC-QUESTIONS 69d).
- The Day carries `stories: []` and `received: []` under `--json`, the fields the reference added with `add story` (1da4a09, RFC 0028 draft) and the shared trip (b57436a, RFC 0025). Only the empty list is read: no fixture has a `story/v1` or `received/v1` line, and the text is unchanged without them (SPEC-QUESTIONS 69e).
- `trips` prints each trip's id as the last part of its row (`· trip:2026-06-15:2026-06-20`), as the reference does since b2009f5 (`logbook trip`, 2026-10-02), so a row names what `logbook trip <id>` and a note can refer to; `tests/fixtures/*/expected-trips/*.txt` are re-captured. The JSON is unchanged.
- `day` reads a stay aboard an asset as a container, as the reference does since e5e08a3 (PR #159, 2026-10-02): the run of consecutive segments aboard one asset, with a stay in it, is one `aboard` row over the whole window read — a single stay aboard too, and a run that began the day before is the same row on the next day, whole — with the segments that touch the day inside it, the run's `points` and `lines`, and its centre (`lat`, `lon`, and the coordinate in the id the night names it by) the inner stay spent longest at; a move aboard with no stay either side stays a move. The night carries a `position` (the stay's `lat` and `lon`; aboard an asset the inner stay that held the longest part of the night window, printed as `aboard Solvind · 60.3000,5.2000 · away`; `null` in transit), the country is read from it, and a night within 400 m of a home place names that place. An unnamed stay with a named place within 5 km is labelled `59.9120,10.7560 near Home, 0.3 km` (the nearest such place, the distance to one decimal) where it printed the coordinates alone. `tests/fixtures/*/expected-day` re-captured from the reference at 36a7d62; SPEC-QUESTIONS 44 and 45 updated.
- `show` spells a number in the generic `key=value` row, nested or not, as RFC 8785 lays it out (`0.000001`, `1e+21`, `100000000000000000000`), as the reference does since b3cd8c5 instead of Python's `repr` (`1e-06`); `src/pyrepr.ts` reuses the chain's `canonicalize`, so the row and the hash can never disagree on a number. Strings, booleans, null, dicts and lists keep Python's spelling. One difference with the reference remains and is written down in SPEC-QUESTIONS 24: an integer from 1e21 is a Python int there and a double here.
- `show` folds one calendar entry that several sources carry as the reference does since dae84b0 (2026-10-01): on the exact start and end, the title compared with case, accents and whitespace aside or the same flight in the title, two or more sources, printed as `×N sources`; before, within five minutes and printed as `ics+ios-calendar` (SPEC-QUESTIONS 28). `tests/fixtures/profiles-sample` grows a third calendar source (77 lines) so the fold and a near miss are both diffed against the reference.
- The vendored conformance sample is `conformance/sample-logbook` of bighydro/logbook at v0.5.0: 31 lines (the first sixteen unchanged, then one line of every profile added since v0.2), head `035a74e0…`. The conformance CI job clones the spec at v0.5.0 and expects that head.
- `src/clock.ts` holds the local-time helpers `show` had (`isDay`, `localizer`, `checkTimezone`) and the new `localMidnight`, `dayAfter` and `daysBetween`; `show` imports them. No behaviour changed.
- SPEC-QUESTIONS 38–41: the hero line and keeper row, a flight without a designator, the two days of the sample that cannot be diffed, and the range, profile and JSON flags, which neither the spec nor the reference defines.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-10-01

### Added
- `verify` and `add` for `logbook/0.2`, written from SPEC.md at v0.3.0 alone: RFC 8785 canonical JSON, SHA-256 chaining and UUIDv7 by hand on Node built-ins; SPEC §6 conformance on the 16-line sample.
- `show --day YYYY-MM-DD [--tz <zone>] [--raw]`: one local day as the reference prints it, every RFC profile summarised, names from the record's own `resolution/v1` lines, runs of points collapsed, calendar entries folded across sources, retractions marked in place; matched to the reference by running it on synthetic records (`tests/fixtures/show-sample`, `tests/fixtures/profiles-sample`), never by reading it, and kept matched by `tests/cross-impl.test.ts`.
- SPEC-QUESTIONS.md: every place the spec left a choice, 1–37.
